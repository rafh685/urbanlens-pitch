Portals show the flat. Not the street.

06 · Competition · our advantage

The map is easy to draw. The data behind it is not.

01

The data work

Each city publishes in its own format, scale and date. We turn it into one comparable street map, and keep it up to date.

02

A score that learns

Every flat a user saves or rejects shows which streets people really prefer. A newcomer starts from zero.

03

Partner, not feature

Portals and agencies can license the layer instead of building it. Integration is part of the plan.

07 · Product · how the score works

Public data in, one personal score out.

01 · Collect

City open data

Noise maps, traffic counts, transit stops, safety statistics, shops and parks.

02 · Clean

One map

Every source placed on the same street grid, with its date and coverage.

03 · Score

0 to 100 per signal

Each street compared with the rest of the city: quiet, safe, transit, walkable.

04 · Match

Your weights

Your priorities count three times more. Same flat, different score for different people.

Honest limit: Spanish safety data is mostly published by district, not by street. We show it at that level, and every layer shows its source and date.

Backup · Data

What the cities publish, and what they don't.

Strong

Noise and traffic

Noise maps building by building in Madrid and Barcelona. 7,360 traffic sensors in Madrid, every 5 minutes. 31 noise stations.

Good

Transit and services

Bus timetables (EMT, TMB) and metro feeds as open data. Shops, parks and schools from open maps.

Limited

Safety

No street-level crime data in Spain. Madrid: police data by district. Barcelona: by police area. We show it at that level.
